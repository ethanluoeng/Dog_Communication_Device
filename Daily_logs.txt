ESP32-s3 devkit Day 1 Checklist:

Specs:
- 2 main processor cores (plus a small ultra-low-power co-processor) 
- Internal memory: On-chip memory that cpu and access very quickly
	- Internal ROM (384 KB) (can't program on it so doesn't really matter)
	- Internal SRAM (512 KB) (volatile memory that can be quickly accessed by the CPU)
	- RTC Memory (16 KB) (Static RAM that's persistent throughout deep sleep)
- External memory: memory on the board
	- Flash: 16 MB
	- PSRAM: 8 MB
	
Chip Boot Control:
	- 4 strapping pins: GPIO0, GPIO3, GPIO45, GPIO46
	- During chip reset, hardware captures samples and stores the voltage level of strapping pins
	- After the reset is released, the strapping pins work as normal-function pins
- Boot Mode Control:
	- GPIO0, GPIO1, GPIO2, and GPIO46 at reset determine the boot mode after the reset is released.
	- SPI Boot mode, Joint Download Boot mode, SPI Download Boot mode (don't really knwo if this matters yet)
	- Wi-Fi and Bluetooth LE can work only when CPU_CLK uses PLL_CLK as its clock source.

Pins: 
- GPIO26–32: BIG NO-NO (SPI flash/PSRAM, base)
- GPIO33–37: ALSO BIG NO-NO (Octal PSRAM extension)

MAX98357A Pinout:
- I2S pins:
	- LRC: Left/Right Clock - this is the pin that tells the amplifier when the data is for the left channel and when its for the right channel
	- BCLK: clock for reading data on the data pin
	- DIN: Data In - both left and right data are sent on this pin
- Other pins:
	- GAIN: Gain pin reads its voltage which corresponds to a specific gain. Connect to GND/Nothing/Vin with resistors to change voltage (look at table for details)
	- SD: pin reads voltage to chose mode: shut down, left, right, or left/2 + right/2
	
- The ESP32-S3 has a GPIO Matrix — a crossbar switch between the chip's internal peripherals (I2S, SPI, UART, etc.) and the physical GPIO pins. Almost any usable GPIO can be assigned to almost any peripheral signal.
- Instead of I2S being hardwired to specific pins, you configure in software which GPIOs map to BCLK, LRC (WS), and DIN.

- I2S is a digital sound protocol that takes PCM digital audio sample data, formatted as signed two’s complement values.
- In a PCM stream, the amplitude of the analog signal is sampled at uniform intervals, and each sample is quantized to the nearest value within a range of digital steps.
- The bit magnitude determines the audio's volume. For 16-bit range: -32,768 to +32,767
- Positive values push the speaker out and negative values push the speaker in! When you rapidly alternate between these two extremes, the speaker cone vibrates back and forth, moving the air to create sound.
- PCM values themselves do not contain inherent frequency information. Frequency is created by how quickly your code feeds these values to the I2S hardware!
- This module takes I2S digital audio input, decodes it into analog, and also amplifies it directly into a speaker.
- Turns out, the reason why it was silent was just because of the wires. All I had to do was switch them for thicker ones and make sure the soldering connection was good. Now it produces a frequency that I can wake up the whole family with.

- I just learned, a good way to picture the electric circuit on MCUs or just in general is think of all the VCC lines as a single conductive plate. They are all connected and if one spot is getting 5v voltage, they all have 5v voltage. There really is no input/output!
- Same with GND, think of all of them as another plate, just that their voltage is always 0.
